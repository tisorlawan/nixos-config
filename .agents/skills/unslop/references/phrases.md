# Phrases to remove

Adapted from stop-slop's `references/phrases.md`.

## Throat-clearing openers

State the content directly. Any "here's what/this/that/why" construction is throat-clearing before the point.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]", "Here's this [X]", "Here's that [X]", "Here's why [X]"
- "Here's what I find interesting"
- "Here's the problem though"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "The truth is,"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"

## Emphasis crutches

These add no meaning. Delete them.

- "Full stop." and "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"

## Business jargon

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, address |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept |
| Landscape (context) | Situation, field |
| Game-changer | Significant, important |
| Double down | Commit, increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis, examination |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Moving forward | Next, from now |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |
| On the same page | Aligned, agreed |

## Adverbs

Cut intensifiers, softeners, and hedges. Keep an adverb that changes the meaning ("only", "not", "first"). If an adverb props up a weak verb, replace the verb.

Common offenders: "really", "just", "literally", "genuinely", "honestly", "simply", "actually", "deeply", "truly", "fundamentally", "inherently", "inevitably", "interestingly", "importantly", "crucially".

Cut these filler phrases too:

- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"

## Meta-commentary

Remove self-referential asides. The text should move without announcing its structure.

- "Hint:", "Plot twist:", "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Dressed up as"
- "The rest of this essay explains..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "In this section, we'll..."
- "As we'll see..."
- "I want to explore..."

## Performative emphasis

False intimacy or manufactured sincerity: "creeps in", "I promise", "They exist, I promise".

## Vague declaratives

A sentence that announces importance without naming the thing. Replace it with the specific thing, or cut it.

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"

## Telling instead of showing

- "This is genuinely hard"
- "This is what leadership actually looks like"
- "actually matters"
